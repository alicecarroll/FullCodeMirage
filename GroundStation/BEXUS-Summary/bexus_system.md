# BEXUS System

## Flight configuration
A typical BEXUS flight uses a 12,000 m3 Zodiac 12 SF helium balloon. The total system mass can exceed 300 kg and the flight train can be more than 100 m long. The payload altitude is typically 25-30 km, with the exact altitude depending on total payload mass.

## Gondola
The primary gondola for BEXUS is the medium Esrange gondola, M-EGON. Its size is about 1.16 m x 1.16 m x 0.84 m and it is designed for payloads up to 100 kg. It uses reinforcement wires that help distribute landing loads and preserve structural integrity. Floor rails are provided for payload fixation, and the gondola can also be mounted using vertical bars or roof bars.

## Tracking
Tracking is mainly provided by EMPIRE, which has its own GPS receiver and transmits the balloon location. E-Link also has GPS. The balloon and payload additionally use air traffic transponders and altitude encoders to aid tracking, and a Globalstar simplex transmitter can be used as redundancy and to support recovery.

## Flight sequence
BEXUS follows the standard stratospheric balloon flight profile.

- Launch: the payload is held by the launch vehicle until helium inflation is complete
- Ascent: nominal ascent is about 5 m/s and takes around 1.5 hours
- Float: altitude typically stabilises at 25-30 km, with float time around 1-5 hours depending on wind and recovery opportunity
- Descent: the cutter separates the balloon, a parachute lowers the payload, and deceleration loads can reach several g
- Landing: landing is planned for sparsely populated areas, usually without lakes, with a nominal landing speed of about 7-8 m/s

During float, altitude variations are usually small, around +/-200 m.

## Flight trajectory
The nominal line-of-sight range between Esrange and the gondola is about 200-300 km, so the flight trajectory is tightly linked to radio visibility and the balloon path. Past flight profiles are used to estimate the mission geometry before launch.

## Recovery
Recovery is normally done by helicopter from the landing site to the nearest road, followed by truck transport back to Esrange. The process can take a couple of days. If time-critical hardware is on board, the team must discuss it in advance with the SSC and ZARM payload managers.
