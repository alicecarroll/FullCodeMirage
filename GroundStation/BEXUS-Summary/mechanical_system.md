# Mechanical System

## Gondola mounting
The BEXUS gondola, M-EGON, provides bottom-bulkhead rails for payload fixation. Experiments can be mounted in four ways:

- floor rails, which are the preferred option
- vertical bars
- roof bars
- other setups, if discussed with SSC well in advance

For floor rails, the payload should use brackets or a bottom plate and is typically bolted with M6 hardware. Vertical and roof bar installations require robust and tight clamp or bolt fixation. Roof bars are also used by the E-Link system, so space and interference must be considered carefully.

Any part of the payload outside the gondola must be secured with a safety cable. The team must prove that the structure and attachment can withstand the relevant loads; there will not be a flight if an item could fall off the gondola.

## Mechanical loads
The main mechanical risks are transport, launch handling, cut-off, landing, helicopter transport, and the return truck journey. The design loads called out in the manual include:

- transient shock of -10 g vertically and +/- 5 g horizontally during parachute opening
- landing velocity of 7 m/s
- unspecified random vibrations during transport phases
- unspecified shocks during transport phases

Landing can be harsher than nominal. On rocky terrain, shocks up to 35 g have been reported, and the gondola may land on its side or upside down.

## Mechanical robustness and recovery
If an experiment protrudes beyond the gondola, sacrificial joints or other contingency measures should be considered to protect both the experiment and the gondola. Water landings are possible but the gondola is not watertight. The payload may also be exposed to snow, mud, and other debris during recovery.

## General design considerations
The manual repeatedly stresses practical engineering issues that directly affect mechanical design:

- accessibility for switches, batteries, and connectors
- availability and procurement of parts
- low mass and controlled size
- cost and manufacturability
- testing effectiveness before transport and flight
- shipping constraints before the campaign

## Mechanical validation
Recommended mechanical verification includes static load testing, shock testing, and vibration testing. The team should validate the structure by analysis and/or test before campaign integration.
