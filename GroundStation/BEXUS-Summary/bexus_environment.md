# BEXUS Environment

## Ground and launch conditions
Before launch, payload integration is normally done at about 20 +/- 5 C. After preparation, the payload is moved outdoors to the launch pad, where September and October temperatures are usually between 0 C and -15 C. Exposure can last several hours.

## Countdown and flight thermal environment
During countdown, experiments can heat up over time, especially during long holds, so temperature regulation should be designed into heat-sensitive modules. In flight, the external air temperature can drop to about -80 C. The lowest temperatures are normally near the tropopause, around 10-20 km, and the stratosphere is usually slightly warmer at float altitude.

Temperatures inside the gondola depend on the experiment configuration, flight time, and float duration. If the sun sets during flight, the balloon can begin to descend as the gas cools.

## Pressure environment
The gondola typically experiences about 5 to 25 mbar during the float phase, and the acceptance level for experiments is 5 mbar. Vacuum-like conditions therefore matter even for payloads that are not sealed vessels.

## Landing and recovery environment
After landing, the payload may remain in snow and cold air for one to two days, typically at 0 C to -15 C. Landing can also expose the experiment to water, mud, soil, vegetation, and unpredictable orientation. The gondola is not watertight.

## RF environment
Esrange requires frequency permission for any transmitter or receiver. The RF environment is tightly controlled because weak satellite signals can be jammed and the site uses several protected bands for safety, telemetry, and recovery. Experiments must avoid all banned bands, including the E-Link band, the EMPIRE/EBASS bands, ATC bands, and the satellite uplink/downlink bands listed in the manual.

## Range and recovery environment
The nominal line-of-sight range between Esrange and the gondola is about 200-300 km, so the balloon environment is a remote tracking and recovery problem as much as a flight problem. Recovery is normally by helicopter, then truck, and can take several days.
