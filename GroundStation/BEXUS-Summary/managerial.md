# Managerial Tasks

## Project organisation
SSC and ZARM payload managers provide technical support during design, manufacturing, integration, and testing. SSC handles campaign management and operations. ZARM provides the DLR service role for experiment integration, testing, and student support in Bremen. ESA mentors also contribute support, and scientific evaluation plus financial support are handled through DLR and SNSA.

Communication between experimenters and the organisers should pass through the SSC and ZARM payload managers. The main contact point depends on the team: ESA Education for SNSA/ESA teams and ZARM for DLR teams.

## Project planning
The programme follows a fixed milestone schedule, roughly from T-16 months to T+8/20 months. The key milestones are:

- T-16 m: call for experiment proposals
- T-12 m: proposal submission deadline
- T-11.5 m: shortlisting
- T-10.5 m: selection workshop and proposal presentations
- T-10 m: final experiment selection
- T-9 m to T-4 m: successive SED versions submitted
- T-7.5 m: Student Training Week and Preliminary Design Review
- T-5 m: Critical Design Review and soldering course
- T-3.5 m: IPR at the experimenters’ organisation
- T-1 m: EAR, final SED, safety inputs, and Ground Safety Plan input
- T+0 m: campaign at Esrange
- T+0.5 m: flight report documentation
- T+1 m: post-flight report distribution by SSC
- T+3 m: SED v5.0 with results
- T+6 m: final report publication
- T+8/20: results seminars

## Documentation responsibilities
The main documents are:

- Student Experiment Documentation (SED): the primary living document for the experiment, submitted in five frozen versions with increasing detail
- Campaign Requirements Plan (CRP): compiled by SSC from the SED and used as the reference for payload descriptions, flight requirements, launch-site requirements, budgets, test activities, and countdown operations
- Ground Safety Plan Questionnaire (GSP-Q): used by the Esrange Safety Board for risk assessment and safety evaluation
- Recovery Sheet: a single A4 page with safe, fast, and simple recovery instructions, illustrated with pictures
- Flight Report Documentation: one to two pages describing in-flight performance and preliminary results, due two weeks after the campaign
- Failure Analysis Report: required if the experiment does not perform as expected and must be included in SED v5.0

## Experimenter role
Teams are responsible for defining the investigation, providing the instrumentation, processing the data, publishing results, and making sure the payload complies with all electrical, mechanical, and safety requirements. Organisers can help, but the team must prove through tests, simulation, and documentation that the experiment is fit and safe for flight.
