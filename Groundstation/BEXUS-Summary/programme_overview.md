# REXUS/BEXUS Programme Overview

## What the programme is
The REXUS/BEXUS programme lets students from universities and higher education colleges across Europe fly scientific and technological experiments on research rockets and balloons. Each year, two rockets and two balloons are launched, carrying up to 20 student-built experiments.

The programme is run under a bilateral agency agreement between DLR and SNSA, with ESA supporting access for students from other European countries. SSC at Esrange is responsible for BEXUS campaign management and operations, while DLR, DLR MORABA, SSC, ZARM, and ESA provide technical support.

## What BEXUS provides
BEXUS is the balloon branch of the programme. Typical BEXUS flights use a 12,000 m3 helium balloon and carry experiments to about 25-30 km altitude for roughly 2-5 hours. The gondola is modular and includes the payload control, telemetry, telecommand, and recovery services needed for the flight.

## The BEXUS flight ticket
To join the launch campaign, a team must pass the Experiment Acceptance Review and receive the BEXUS flight ticket. That ticket includes:

- general management and planning of the BEXUS project
- provision of the balloon vehicle and supporting systems
- pre-flight testing of payload telemetry, telecommand, and flight simulation
- assembly of the payload into the gondola at Esrange
- provision of laboratory facilities at the launch site
- daytime launch, operations, piloting, and recovery
- data acquisition, including quick-look and real-time data
- disassembly of the payload and return to the experimenters
- a BEXUS campaign report

## What experiment teams should remember
The programme is highly structured and safety-driven. Teams are expected to design, build, test, document, and operate their payloads so that they fit the interfaces, survive the flight environment, and can be recovered safely. Good communication with the organisers and timely delivery of documentation are essential.
